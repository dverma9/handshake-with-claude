Day 2
Publish Post #1 — the profile rebuild post
This post serves two purposes: it announces your new positioning and drives comments (which signal the algorithm to expand your reach). Copy, personalise lightly, publish.
I just did something uncomfortable. I let AI brutally roast my LinkedIn profile. My headline had 11 pipe-separated words — including "Immediate Joiner." My About section had zero numbers in 500 words. My experience bullets read like a job description from 2010. 15 years of engineering leadership. Hidden in plain sight. Here's what changed: → Headline now says what I actually do for clients, not what I am → About section opens with a client problem, not my job title → Every experience bullet leads with an action verb and ends with a result If you're a Technical Manager or senior engineer and your profile still says things like "passionate about technology" or "results-driven professional" — fix it today. Here's the truth: your LinkedIn profile is either working for you 24/7 or it's silently costing you opportunities. I was in the second camp. Now I'm not. What's the worst line currently sitting on your LinkedIn? Drop it in the comments — let's roast it together.
~940 characters — well within the 1,300 limit

Hook strategy: Opens with vulnerability ("uncomfortable") which stops the scroll. The specifics ("11 pipe-separated words") are funny and relatable. The CTA asks for comments — "drop it in the comments" invites participation which feeds the algorithm.

Day 3
Send 10 strategic connection requests
Do not connect randomly. Every connection should be someone who could hire you or refer you within the next 6 months. Quality over volume.
Who to target (in this order)
CTOs at 50–500 person tech companies (India, UK, US) VP Engineering / Head of Engineering Startup founders in Telecom, Healthcare, Fintech Tech consulting firm owners Freelance talent managers / staffing leads Project delivery heads at IT service companies
Connection note template — under 300 characters
"Hi [Name] — I'm a freelance Technical Manager specialising in Java/Microservices delivery. I work with engineering teams navigating complex projects. Came across your profile and thought it worth connecting — your work in [their industry] is interesting."

Always personalise the last sentence with something specific to their profile or company. Generic notes get ignored. Specific notes get accepted and sometimes replied to.

Day 4
Comment on 5 posts in your target space
Find 5 posts by CTOs, engineering leads, or startup founders in Telecom, Healthcare, or Fintech. Apply the Value Comment Formula to each one.
Step 1 — Agree
Validate their point genuinely in 1 sentence. Do not start with "Great post!" — that's noise. Be specific about what you agree with and why.
Step 2 — Add insight
Share a specific experience you've had that adds to their point. 2 sentences max. The more specific and domain-relevant, the better — this is where your 15 years and 7 industries become a superpower.
Step 3 — Ask a question
End with a question that invites them to respond. This triggers a notification to them, starts a conversation, and puts your name in front of their network when they reply.
Example comment in action
"This is exactly right — sprint planning breakdowns almost always start with unclear acceptance criteria, not poor velocity. In my experience leading Healthcare IT teams, we fixed this by introducing a 'definition of ready' gate before any story entered sprint planning. Took 2 weeks to embed but cut mid-sprint scope changes by roughly half. Have you found any formats that make the product owner / engineering handoff cleaner?"
Day 5
Publish Post #2 — hot-take / lessons learned
This post establishes your expertise and point of view. It should feel like something only someone with your specific experience could write. Publish as-is or adapt with your own example.
Hot take: most software projects don't fail because of the technology. They fail because nobody is managing the humans. In 15 years across 7 industries, I've seen the same pattern repeat: → Talented developers. Unclear scope. Nobody owning stakeholder expectations. → Result: a 6-month project becoming a 14-month project, at 2x the budget. Kafka doesn't cause delays. Microservices don't cause scope creep. People do. The most underrated skill in software engineering isn't system design or code review. It's being able to walk into a client meeting and say — clearly, calmly, early — "we have a problem, here's what it is, here's how we fix it." Most engineers wait until the problem is visible. By then, the damage is done. I've spent 15 years being the person who says it early. That's not a technical skill. That's a leadership skill. What's the earliest warning sign you've learned to watch for in a project before things go wrong?
~1,020 characters — within the 1,300 limit

Why this works: Contrarian opening grabs attention. The specific pain (6-month project → 14 months) is visceral and recognisable. It positions you as someone who combines technical depth with leadership maturity — exactly the profile a client wants to hire. The question at the end is open enough to attract engineers AND CTOs.

Day 6
Engage on your posts + 5 more connections
Reply to every single comment on both posts today — even one-liners. "Great point — I've also seen this when..." is enough. LinkedIn's algorithm treats replies as fresh engagement signals and re-circulates the post. Do not let comments go unanswered for more than 24 hours. Then send 5 more connection requests using the Day 3 template, targeting anyone who liked or commented on your posts but isn't connected yet.
Day 7
Review your numbers — know what they mean
Go to LinkedIn Analytics (top of your profile → "Analytics" tab). Check these three numbers and interpret them using the guide below.
Profile views up
Your content is driving traffic. People are curious enough after seeing your posts to check who you are. Keep posting consistently — this number compounds over weeks, not days.
Connection acceptance below 40%
Your note is too generic or the target isn't right. Try personalising the last sentence more specifically — reference a post they published or a company milestone. Also try connecting with 2nd-degree connections (shared connection = more trust).
Post impressions below 300
Your hook isn't strong enough. The first line is the only thing visible in the feed — it needs to create a pattern interrupt. Try opening with a provocative number, a counterintuitive claim, or a short one-liner that creates curiosity. Post at 8–9am IST on Tuesday, Wednesday, or Thursday for best reach.
No inbound messages yet
Normal at day 7. Inbound from content takes 4–8 weeks of consistent posting to build. The goal this week was to lay the foundation. Stay consistent — 2 posts per week, 5 value comments per day — for 30 days and your profile views and connection requests will compound significantly.
Appearing in search results
Check "Search appearances" in Analytics. If it's climbing, your skills and keywords are working. If it's flat, go back and make sure you've used the exact skill node names as listed in the rebuild — LinkedIn's search indexes exact skill matches, not synonyms.
