Section 1 — Headline (3 options)

Formula: [Role/Identity] | [Who you help or what you do] | [Proof or differentiator]

Option 1 — keyword optimized
Freelance Technical Manager | Java · Spring Boot · Microservices · Kafka · AWS | 15 Years Delivering Complex Software Across 7 Industries
Why this works: LinkedIn's search algorithm heavily weights your headline. When a CTO searches "freelance Java technical manager" or "microservices delivery consultant," this headline surfaces you. Every major keyword is present. "15 years, 7 industries" is a number-backed differentiator that no generic headline has. The pipe separators create visual scan-stops so a reader catches the keywords even at speed.

When to use: If you want inbound from LinkedIn search — clients who are actively hunting for someone like you.
Option 2 — value proposition
I Help Startups & Enterprises Ship Complex Java Projects On Time | Freelance Technical Manager | Spring Boot · Microservices · AWS
Why this works: "Ship complex Java projects on time" hits the exact pain of any CTO or founder who has experienced late delivery. It speaks directly to the client's problem, not your job title. Starting with "I help" makes it instantly client-facing — it's a value statement, not a credential statement. The tech stack follows for credibility.

When to use: If your primary strategy is posting content and having clients visit your profile — they'll see this value prop first and immediately understand what you solve.
Option 3 — authority / thought leadership
Senior Technical Manager | Turning Engineering Chaos Into Predictable Delivery | Java · Microservices · AWS | 15 Years · Telecom · Healthcare · Fintech
Why this works: "Engineering Chaos Into Predictable Delivery" is a positioning line — memorable, specific, and repeatable. You can build every post and article around this theme. The domain list (Telecom, Healthcare, Fintech) signals depth of experience to a client who operates in one of those spaces. This headline makes you sound like someone worth following.

When to use: If you plan to post consistently and want to build a personal brand alongside client acquisition.
My recommendation for your goal (attracting clients): Start with Option 2. It speaks directly to a client's pain and is the most compelling cold-read. Once you have 3–4 posts published and some engagement, switch to Option 3 to reinforce the authority positioning.
Section 2 — About section (complete rewrite)

The About section is your sales page. Every line has a job to do. Here's the structure and the copy.

Lines 1–2 — The hook (visible before "see more" — most critical)

If your software project is behind schedule, over-scoped, or being held together by heroics — that's the exact situation I've spent 15 years fixing.

Strategy: Opens with the client's problem, not your credentials. The word "heroics" is specific — any CTO who has had engineers working weekends to save a launch will feel this. It earns the click to "see more" because they want to know who's talking.

Lines 3–5 — The story (what you do, who you help, why it matters)

I'm a freelance Technical Manager and Java architect with 15 years of hands-on experience building and leading engineering teams that actually deliver.

I work with startups and mid-size companies that need an experienced hand to structure their delivery, manage complex engineering squads, and ship software that doesn't drift — across Java, Spring Boot, Microservices, Kafka, and AWS.

I've worked across 7 industries: Telecom, Healthcare, Hospitality, Retail, Insurance, Clinical systems, and Automation. That means I understand what "complex" looks like in your domain — not just in a textbook.

Strategy: "Who actually deliver" and "doesn't drift" are pointed phrases — they imply you've seen teams that don't deliver and projects that do drift. Clients with that experience will nod. The 7 industries list is powerful social proof that takes 3 seconds to read and establishes enormous breadth.

Lines 6–8 — Proof (achievements and numbers) fill in your real numbers

Over 15 years I have:
— Led engineering teams of [8–15] developers across [X] concurrent Java projects
— Served as Scrum Master, managing sprints, backlog, and stakeholder delivery reviews
— Built and maintained client relationships across [N] enterprise accounts in [India / UK / US]
— Mentored [N] junior-to-senior engineers and designed technical training programmes
— Delivered projects across domains including Telecom OSS/BSS, Healthcare EHR, and Retail POS systems

Strategy: Bullet format here is intentional — it's scannable. A client skimming after clicking "see more" will catch the numbers and the domain names before they decide to read the full text. Replace every bracketed item with your closest real estimate. Even approximate numbers ("teams of roughly 10–12") are infinitely better than nothing.

Last line — CTA (call to action)

If you need a senior technical partner to lead your next project — not just advise on it — send me a message. I take on freelance and consulting engagements where I can make a real difference to delivery outcomes.

Strategy: "Lead your next project — not just advise on it" is a deliberate differentiation. Many consultants advise. You lead. That's a more valuable and more expensive engagement, and it attracts clients who need a doer, not a commentator. The CTA is specific: message me. Always end with one action.

Section 3 — Experience — top entry (full bullet rewrites)

Title: Technical Manager  |  Background: 15 years Java Software Development

Rule: Every bullet = Action verb + Specific context + Measurable result

Before

Led multiple development teams

After

Led cross-functional Java engineering teams of [8–15] developers across [X] concurrent projects — managing delivery timelines, technical quality, and weekly stakeholder reporting.

Why the change: "Multiple" is meaningless to a client. Team size, concurrency, and the fact that you owned stakeholder reporting tells a client exactly what they'd be getting. Fill in your real team size.
Before

Played as Scrum Master, JIRA Management

After

Drove Agile delivery as Scrum Master across [N] active squads — owned sprint planning, backlog grooming, velocity tracking, and JIRA workflow management, reducing average sprint carry-over by [X]%.

Why the change: "Played as" signals you weren't committed to the role — it sounds like a temporary hat you wore. "Drove" is an action verb that signals ownership. Adding sprint carry-over as a metric shows you actually improved delivery, not just ran ceremonies.
Before

Contributed in development of complex application

After

Architected and delivered complex distributed systems using Java, Spring Boot, and Microservices — [e.g. a Telecom billing platform processing X transactions/day / a Healthcare EHR module serving X concurrent users].

Why the change: "Contributed" is the weakest possible verb — it could mean you wrote one function. "Architected and delivered" positions you as the person who owned the outcome. Add the domain and scale of the system — this is where your 7-industry experience becomes concrete proof.
Before

Client Management, Stake Holder Management

After

Managed client relationships across [N] enterprise accounts — conducted weekly delivery reviews, owned escalation handling, and maintained [high / X%] client satisfaction and retention across multi-year engagements.

Why the change: Two nouns with no verb aren't bullets — they're categories. "Managed client relationships across N accounts" immediately tells a client you can handle their account too. Multi-year engagements signals trust and continuity, which is exactly what a new client wants to hear.
Before

Strong debugging skills, mentoring, provided technical trainings

After

Mentored [N] junior-to-senior engineers and designed structured technical training programmes covering Java architecture, Microservices design patterns, and Agile best practices — cutting onboarding time by [X] weeks.

Why the change: "Strong debugging skills" is a self-assessment — every developer says this. What matters to a client is that you can build teams, not just debug code. The rewrite shows you invest in your team's growth, which signals maturity and leadership, not just technical ability.
Before

Experienced in multiple domains — Telecom, Hospitality, Retail, Healthcare & Insurance, Clinical, Automation, AI

After

Delivered mission-critical projects across 7 industries: Telecom (OSS/BSS), Healthcare (EHR/clinical systems), Retail (POS/inventory), Hospitality, Insurance, Automation, and AI — bringing domain-specific context to every engagement.

Why the change: Adding the sub-domain in brackets (OSS/BSS, EHR, POS) takes 4 words but doubles the credibility — it proves you know what you're talking about in each sector, not just that you once worked there. "Bringing domain-specific context" is the client benefit: they don't have to educate you on their industry.
